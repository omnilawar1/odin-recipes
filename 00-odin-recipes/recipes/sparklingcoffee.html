Sparkling Coffee

An effervescent artisan cold brew elegantly infused with fine carbonation, offering a deeply roasted aroma and a vibrant, crisp finish.

Ingredients

    Single-Origin Coffee Beans: 20g (Light-to-medium roast, preferably with bright, fruity, or floral notes like an Ethiopian Yirgacheffe). Filtered Water: 150ml Chilled Mineral Water or Tonic: 100ml (High-carbonation, neutral-tasting) Ice: Hand-carved (to minimise dillution) Garnish: A single, expressed zest of an organic orange or a sprig of fresh, aromatic mint to heighten the sensory experience.

Steps

    Precision Extraction: Select a high-altitude, single-origin bean to ensure a profile with bright, natural acidity. Prepare a double-strength concentrate (1:7 ratio) using a slow, manual pour-over method to capture delicate floral and fruit aromatics, avoiding any bitterness. Thermal Conditioning: Immediately shock-cool the hot concentrate by swirling it in a chilled stainless steel vessel nested in ice. This "flash-chilling" locks in the volatile aromatics that are often lost during slow cooling. The Vessel & Ice: Select a thin-rimmed, high-clarity crystal tumbler. Place a single, precision-carved, slow-melting clear ice cube in the base to ensure optimal surface-area-to-volume ratio, preventing premature dilution while maintaining sub-zero temperatures. The Effervescent Pour: Gently introduce premium, mineral-rich sparkling water—chilled to exactly 2°C—directly over the ice cube to preserve maximum carbonation. The goal is to retain the "bead" of the bubbles for the duration of the service. Layered Integration: Using a weighted bar spoon, slowly drizzle the room-temperature coffee concentrate down the stem of the spoon into the sparkling water. This technique maintains the visual separation, creating a beautiful gradient (ombré) effect from deep amber to pale gold. Aromatic Finishing: Perform an "expression" by twisting a swath of organic orange peel just above the glass, releasing the citrus essential oils across the surface. Secure the zest to the rim as a final, minimalist visual element, serving the drink immediately before the carbonation dissipates.
